The argument

Why two wavelengths.

  1. Red stays near the surface.

    At 660 nm, light is absorbed in the epidermis and dermis. It is the wavelength most often studied for skin tone and texture.

  2. Near-infrared travels further.

    At 850 nm, light is invisible and reaches deeper tissue. That is why it appears in research on muscle recovery and joint comfort.

  3. The mechanism is cellular.

    Researchers link both to cytochrome c oxidase, an enzyme in the mitochondria involved in producing cellular energy (Hamblin, AIMS Biophysics, 2017).

  4. The skin evidence is controlled.

    A trial of 136 volunteers reported improved skin complexion and skin feeling after twice-weekly sessions (Wunsch and Matuschka, Photomed Laser Surg, 2014).

These are summaries of published research, not medical claims.

Questions

Asked often.

Is it safe for my eyes?
The LEDs emit no UV. The light is bright, so goggles are included and you should not stare into the panel.
Will it tan or burn?
It will not tan. Red and near-infrared light contain no UV.
Why do some LEDs look dim?
Those are the 850 nm LEDs. Near-infrared sits outside human vision, so they glow faintly at full output.
