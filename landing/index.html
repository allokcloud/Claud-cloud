The science

Two wavelengths. Two depths.

Red and near-infrared sit in the skin's "optical window", where light is absorbed and scattered less. Researchers link the effect, called photobiomodulation, to cytochrome c oxidase in your mitochondria.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Can't find yours? Ask in the quote form and a specialist will reply.

Is it safe for my eyes?+

The LEDs emit no UV. Red light is very bright, so we include protective goggles and recommend you don't stare into the panel.

Will it tan or burn my skin?+

No. Red and near-infrared light contain no UV, so they can't tan or burn. The panel feels warm, not hot.

Why do the 850 nm LEDs look dim?+

Near-infrared is outside human vision. Those LEDs glow faintly but are running at full output.
