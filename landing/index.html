The science

Two wavelengths, two depths.

Photobiomodulation uses specific red and near-infrared wavelengths that fall inside the "optical window", where skin absorbs and scatters less light. Researchers link the effect to cytochrome c oxidase in the mitochondria.

660 nm, red

Absorbed near the surface. Commonly studied for skin tone, texture and fine lines.

850 nm, near-infrared

Travels further into tissue. Commonly studied for muscle recovery and joint comfort.

Bars show relative depth, not measured millimetres. Reference: Hamblin MR, "Mechanisms and applications of the anti-inflammatory effects of photobiomodulation," AIMS Biophysics, 2017.

FAQ

Common questions.

Is it safe for my eyes?

The LEDs emit no UV. Red light is very bright, so we include protective goggles and recommend you don't look directly into the panel.

Will it tan or burn my skin?

No. Red and near-infrared light don't contain UV, so they don't tan or burn. The panel stays warm to the touch, not hot.

Why can I barely see the 850 nm LEDs?

Near-infrared is outside human vision. Those LEDs glow faintly but are working at full output.
